It's around 00:44. I was working on improving my company's DAG pipelines and while fixing some of the bugs on integrations, a LinkedIn notif pops up, something about builders and founders; I've been tinkering with [Radicle](https://radicle.dev/) so something related to that also pops up because privacy is just dead. Seeing these notifs, A... thought popped up in my mind.

I've been thinking lately, in almost every aspect of modern life, I've observed that be it individuals or corporations, we've optimized ourselves into monotony; maybe it's just the circles I'm within. People are "building" something or themselves, but nobody I know really (and I mean _really_) just creates for the love of it. It's a hard thing to describe, so bear with me.

The media that I've consumed, how exactly does it influence my soul? I've been revisiting some of my all time faves in Anime, very niche stuff sometimes and some of the popular shows like `Jobless Reincarnation` or `Somali and the Forest Spirit` and I realized that quite a lot of things that touched my heart and soul were without rhyme or reason. It might've been one dialog or some scene, perhaps the music or even just a single screenshot that makes it worth to re-experience the show.

Now that I think about it, it's the same with any media I've consumed thus far. The entire show/book/movie can suck absolute ass, but there's always that _one_ thing that makes you burst into a thousand motes of different emotions. Funny isn't it? I'm almost moved into having tears of... idk what while I type this.

It's quite interesting, us humans I mean, so full of silent desperation, so infinitely finite. I recall clearly, one of the things that has stuck with me for the longest times; (also im going on a mega rant here idk im in a mood lmao)

There's this book series I've been following for quite a _while_ now called [Painting The Mists by Patrik G. Laplantie](https://paintingthemists.com/). I've read the entire series and re-read it quite a few times, it's not even anything _special_ per se. For a lot of people, it might be considered the same ol' [cultivation slop](https://www.reddit.com/r/ProgressionFantasy/comments/1r177rk/cultivation_slop/) (sorry if you dunno the term idk google it?). But one of the reasons that the series has stuck with me (btw it doesnt take the top spot on my all time favourites in terms of books, because it has it's own spot) is because I feel a very strange similarity to the MC in that series. I haven't a clue on what the Patrik's intent was when he was writing the books, nor do I remember when I first read the first book, but what I do know is that _one_ line from the first book _stirred_ my soul.

It's quite simple really:

```spoiler Painting The Mists
the MC comes across a staff/brush that reads out:

_Do not let worry color the landscape_.
_Why not paint the heavens?_
_With this Clear Sky Brush._

```

I don't know why it affected me so, but just reading this line, as I was and will be, always gives me a window into what Patrik was thinking and wanting to convey to us readers. To be unfettered truly.

And it's not just this series. The same thing happens elsewhere. In the recent Dune movies, when Paul gives his speech amidst the fremen and says: "By the Hand of God, I'm Mahdi", _that_ touched my soul. Or in my all time favourite book series, ["_The Wheel of Time_" by Robert Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wheel_of_Time), the interactions of Perrin with Faile. Or [His Dark Materials by Phillip Pullman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Dark_Materials) and Lyra's affection for Iorek. These moments make it worth reading through _books_ of text.

So (as I have before and shall continue to do so) I now ask myself... _why_? Just _why_ is this the case? The reasoning remains the same, it's art. That's what art _does_. Heck, I even have a quote on the left of my blogs that's about just the same. I can't quite remember where I picked this quote up, but it's been with me for a long while as well:

> Art is not just meant as a medium to express oneself. \
> _Art_ is something you do to show others how broken you are, so that they may not feel as alone.

_That_ is true art. Almost sounds and feels _religious_ doesn't it? Funny how that is.

Not all of it might speak to everyone, but as an artist if your work touches the soul of even _one_ more person, that's just enough imo.

People say that we're (mostly) the sum of our experiences, nothing more. If that's true to whatever degree, then it also stands to reason that the experiences that touch our soul in the deepest sense also contribute to that '_sum_' in a greater way than something we did on a random tuesday. And I think that's what separates _creation_ from just making things.

_Creation_ is... _pure_. It doesn't quite care about the creator or even about the "_value_" it creates. The intent matters to a large extent but the act of _creating_ something is an art unto itself. However on the other hand: "building" is for the sake of something. It's filled with some intent, yes, and it might also be considered an art to some extent if done with the correct intent and structure, but unlike _creation_ it's **not** an art unto itself. It needs an observer, the act of building demands a consumer.

What I've been observing is that, as a species, we've moved quite a bit towards consumerism. This seems to me, to be happening to such an extent that the degree to which people _build_ vs the degree to which people _create_ has started to diverge and very much only tilt towards _building_. Once again, maybe I'm just biased, or my experiences up to this point in my life tell me an incomplete picture. I am, after all, one person. I can't hold the entire macrocosm's worth of information in my tiny brain, but I stand by what I say.

This constant need to _build_ instead of _creating_ makes societies, countries, communities and ultimately, people feel very much hollow. Haven't you noticed it yourself dear reader, how _good_ it feels to help a fellow human being without expecting something in return? To feel the warmth of another soul?

Istg this rant makes feel like some very right winged guy's gonna call me a "sissy liberal", but looking beyond the bullshit divisions that politics instills into our society there's an argument to be made here.

There's artists out there, but how many of them are _truly_ creating? It's easy to produce art but it's _very_ difficult to produce **art**. Yes beauty is in the heart of the beholder but like come on, both you and I know that _true_ art, stuff that's monumental has a different _taste_ to it. It's got _**depth**_ to it in some sense.

> [!note] Also like I'm in _no_ way downplaying or shitting on artists out there bruh chill tf out readers

I don't think the difference is _talent_, or _effort_, or even taste in the traditional sense, I think it's more of who the thing is _for_. The moment we make something for a metric, or a portfolio, or a launch, or maybe an audience that hasn't shown up yet, we've quietly handed the steering wheel to a 3rd party. The work still gets done and it might even be good, but then it's shaped by the shape of the hole it's meant to fill, and holes are dug by other people.

Creation doesn't have that problem because it doesn't have that dependency. You make the thing, and if it resonates with someone, wonderful, and if it doesn't, who gives a shit? what matters is that it still came into existence. Patrik didn't write that line for _me_ specifically, I believe it was just him reflecting his thoughts and feelings out loud for whatever reasons he had, could've been just money but it doesn't quite matter now does it? What matters is that it just fell out of somebody being honest on a page and I happened to walk past at the right angle and at the right time.

So. Long ass rant right? Well, I hope it was somewhat enjoyable, but more importantly I hope that this made you _think_ about the nuance between `building` and _`creating`_.
